Reflection Journal, Module Three

When the Image
Changes the Story

Fall 2026 • 4 minute read

This week changed the way I think about how much work an image can do in a piece of writing.

For the comic analysis, I chose a Calvin and Hobbes strip in which Calvin imagines himself as Spaceman Spiff. Most of the comic presents an elaborate science-fiction adventure involving a crash landing, toxic fumes, and a gelatinous creature. Only in the final panel do we realize that the entire adventure is Calvin’s imaginative response to cafeteria tapioca.

What interested me most was how many different modes were working together to create that reveal. The text gives the imaginary world its dramatic tone. Spiff is not simply uncomfortable. He is trapped on a hostile planet facing dangerous conditions. Color reinforces that feeling through the strange yellows, greens, and oranges of the alien landscape. Layout controls the pace of the sequence by moving between large establishing panels and smaller moments of action. Even the spaces between panels matter because the reader has to infer what happens between one image and the next.

For me, though, the images carry the most weight. If the final panel only said that Calvin thought the cafeteria food was disgusting, the joke would not have nearly the same effect. Seeing Calvin sitting at the table holding the tapioca forces me to reinterpret all of the images that came before it. The alien slime suddenly becomes cafeteria food, the toxic atmosphere becomes the smell of lunch, and Spaceman Spiff becomes Calvin’s way of transforming an ordinary experience into something much larger.

That realization also connects to the literacy narrative I am developing. I started this course with a tendency to think of images as something added to writing. I am beginning to understand that images, layout, sequence, and even the spaces between images can carry meaning that words cannot communicate as efficiently. In this comic, the image does not illustrate the message. The image helps create the message.
