Module 14 • Reflection, Revision, and Portfolio Building

From Intuitive Communication to
Intentional Composition

Portfolio Preparation

November 27, 2026 • 6 minute read

As I begin planning my final portfolio for ENG 306T, I have been considering how to represent my development throughout the semester without simply assembling a chronological collection of completed assignments. Rather than treating the portfolio as an inventory of academic accomplishments, I intend to develop a cohesive reflective narrative examining how my understanding of multimodal communication has evolved and how that development connects to my existing professional experience.

I entered this course with an established background in military service, executive leadership, enterprise technology, and entrepreneurship, all of which required substantial experience in strategic communication. Consequently, the central theme of my portfolio will not be the acquisition of communication skills from an entirely unfamiliar starting point. Instead, I intend to explore the transition from intuitive communication to increasingly intentional composition, examining how the course encouraged me to become more conscious of the rhetorical, cognitive, visual, and structural decisions involved in presenting information.

Kathleen Blake Yancey's (1998) discussion of reflection provides a useful theoretical foundation for this approach. Her distinction between reflection-in-action, constructive reflection, and reflection-in-presentation offers a framework for examining individual composing decisions, identifying patterns of development across multiple assignments, and communicating that cumulative learning to an external audience. Applying this framework will allow me to move beyond describing what I created and instead examine how and why my approaches developed throughout the semester.

My existing Literacy in Progress website will provide both the foundation for the portfolio and an artifact of learning in its own right. Because the website has evolved alongside my coursework, selected excerpts and historical design comparisons can illustrate changes in how I organize, present, and reflect upon information. I also anticipate incorporating selected examples from The Elsinore Files, my interactive literary remediation project, and Beyond the Streak, my examination of participatory branding and digital persuasion through Duolingo's social media presence. These projects offer distinct opportunities to evaluate audience agency, rhetorical design, evidence-based communication, and the role of revision in multimodal composition.

My final social media campaign, AnewSphere: Own Your Sphere, will provide an opportunity to examine the synthesis of these concepts and their transfer into entrepreneurial practice. I also intend to draw selectively from my BLST 101 prior-learning portfolio to establish the professional and academic experiences that informed my approach at the beginning of the semester.

Organizationally, I envision an integrated reflective essay supported by curated visual evidence, project excerpts, documented revisions, and relevant course scholarship. Artifact selection will be guided by each composition's ability to demonstrate meaningful development rather than solely its level of polish or academic performance.

Ultimately, my objective is to construct a portfolio that demonstrates not only what I produced throughout ENG 306T but also how my understanding of communication became more deliberate, reflective, and transferable. The portfolio itself should embody the principles of intentional multimodal composition that it examines.

Reference

Yancey, K. B. (1998). Reflection in the writing classroom. Utah State University Press. https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/usupress_pubs/120
