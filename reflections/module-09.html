Module 9 • Reflection Journal

From Narrative Concept to
Multimodal Experience

Storyboarding narrative structure, visual design, and audience interaction for The Elsinore Files.

Fall 2026 • 4 minute read

This week marked an important transition in my multimodal writing project as I moved from developing the concept for The Elsinore Files to determining how that concept will function as a cohesive digital narrative. My project reimagines Shakespeare's Hamlet as an investigative case file, inviting audiences to examine evidence, consider conflicting perspectives and construct their own interpretations of the events at Elsinore. The challenge now is translating that concept into a storyboard that does more than organize information. It needs to demonstrate how narrative structure, visual design and audience interaction work together to create meaning.

One of my most important takeaways from Joe Lambert's Digital Storytelling Cookbook (2010) is that effective multimedia storytelling requires deliberate choices about what information to reveal, when to reveal it and which medium communicates it most effectively. Lambert's discussion of narrative economy reinforced something I encountered while developing my storyboard: adding more material does not necessarily create a stronger experience. In fact, too many visual elements, explanations or transitions can distract from the central narrative. My next step is to refine the storyboard so that each frame serves a specific purpose, whether introducing evidence, establishing context, complicating an interpretation or advancing the investigation.

I am also reconsidering how the audience experiences uncertainty. Hamlet is fundamentally concerned with questions of truth, perception, motivation and the reliability of information. Rather than resolving those questions for the audience, I want the investigative format to preserve their complexity. This requires careful attention to sequencing and pacing. The order in which evidence appears will influence what readers believe, while visual hierarchy and document design will shape which details they notice first. I plan to evaluate each storyboard frame not only for what it communicates individually but also for how it changes the audience's understanding of the material that came before it.

Another priority is ensuring that the project's multimodal elements contribute meaning rather than simply create atmosphere. Archival-style documents, evidence labels, typography and visual transitions should reinforce the investigative experience without overwhelming the content. I also want to consider where audio or interactive elements could add something that static text cannot. However, those decisions need to remain grounded in accessibility and usability. An effective design should encourage exploration without making the audience work unnecessarily hard to understand the narrative.

Moving forward, my immediate priorities are to finalize the storyboard's narrative sequence, refine the visual hierarchy, identify the multimedia assets needed for production and evaluate how the audience will navigate the experience. I also intend to use the feedback from my initial proposal to ensure that the storyboard remains focused and achievable. Ultimately, I want The Elsinore Files to demonstrate that multimodal storytelling is not simply the presentation of a familiar narrative through different media. It is an opportunity to reconsider how audiences encounter information, evaluate competing interpretations and participate in constructing meaning.

Reference

Lambert, J. (2010). Digital storytelling cookbook. Digital Diner Press.
