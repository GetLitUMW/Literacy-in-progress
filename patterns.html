Module 10

Testing What the Design
Asks Readers to Do

Earlier reflections examined how presentation shapes meaning. Building The Elsinore Files brings that question into a working experience: comparison can expose unequal perspectives, while preserved notebook entries make reconsideration visible. A new tension emerges between supporting readers and directing their conclusions. The next step is to examine readers’ explanations rather than assuming that a functioning interface demonstrates learning.

Perspective and power

Uses comparison to reveal how unequal knowledge and consequences shape the meaning of an encounter.

Multimodal purpose

Evaluates whether images, audio, and interaction contribute distinct interpretive work rather than simply adding atmosphere.

Visible reconsideration

Preserves initial and revised notebook entries so the reasoning behind a judgment remains available for reflection.

Guidance and agency

Questions whether labels and prompts support close reading or direct readers too strongly toward an expected conclusion.

Evidence of learning

Plans a small voluntary pilot focused on readers’ explanations without claiming that the draft has already demonstrated its intended outcomes.

Module 09

From Analysis
to Narrative Design

Module Nine marks a shift from analyzing multimodal design to constructing it intentionally. Developing The Elsinore Files requires decisions about narrative sequence, perspective, and how evidence shapes interpretation. Lambert's principles of narrative economy reinforce that effective storytelling depends not on the quantity of information or media, but on how deliberately each element contributes to meaning.

Narrative design

Uses sequencing, perspective, and controlled disclosure to shape interpretation and guide the audience through an investigative narrative.

Multimodal literacy

Assigns distinct rhetorical purposes to text, visuals, sound, and interaction rather than treating multimedia elements as decorative additions.

Critical literacy

Recognizes how the selection, presentation, and ordering of evidence influence judgments about credibility, motivation, and meaning.

Audience agency

Designs opportunities for readers to investigate competing perspectives and construct interpretations rather than passively receive predetermined conclusions.

Reflective practice

Applies Joe Lambert's principles of narrative economy, pacing, and meaningful multimodal composition alongside instructor feedback to refine the project's scope and storytelling approach.

Module 08

Evidence
Needs Context

Module Eight reframes remediation as a series of interpretive choices about what survives, what becomes prominent, and what an audience must supply. Planning The Elsinore Files connects earlier work on proximity and ethical information design to a new responsibility: preserving context while inviting participants to reconsider how presentation shaped their judgments.

Critical literacy

Evaluates credibility, omissions, perspective, and the context that can disappear when a dramatic scene becomes separate records.

Multimodal literacy

Connects each proposed mode to a storytelling purpose and asks what meaning might be gained or lost in translation.

Digital literacy

Distinguishes clicking through documents from meaningful participation and plans a sequence that can prompt reconsideration.

Ethical awareness

Preserves character agency, resists treating Ophelia and Gertrude only as evidence about Hamlet, and distinguishes adaptation from source material.

Accessibility

Builds transcripts, relevant image descriptions, readable text, and clear navigation into the proposal from the beginning.

Module 07

Reading
the Design

Analyzing my own website shifted my attention from identifying design choices to examining how those choices shape interpretation. I now recognize that navigation, visual indicators, and page arrangement can make learning visible while also making it appear more orderly or measurable than it is. My focus is moving toward explaining design decisions, acknowledging uncertainty, and distinguishing intended effects from demonstrated reader responses.

Rhetorical analysis

Connects specific design choices to their interpretive consequences.

Reading paths

Recognizes how latest-first ordering frames earlier reflections.

Critical visual literacy

Questions the apparent certainty of subjective visual indicators.

Reflective practice

Identifies limitations and possible revisions without claiming they are already resolved.

A Shift in How I Document Learning

Earlier modules use subjective visual indicators to record emerging patterns in my literacy development. As my understanding of multimodal rhetoric evolved, I began recognizing that progress bars could suggest a level of precision that reflective observations cannot support.

Beginning with Module 7, I shifted toward qualitative descriptions that better capture the complexity, uncertainty, and context of my learning. This change reflects a growing awareness that the way information is presented influences how audiences interpret it.
